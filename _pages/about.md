What turns ordinary weather into an extreme? and why hot extremes are becoming more frequent ? I study the circulation patterns behind weather extremes such as heatwaves, cold spells and storms. I analyse how atmosphere’s dynamics and thermodynamics shapes these weather extremes. Using observation-based datasets, model simulations and Lagrangian transport methods to identify processes involved. 
The better we understand these circulations, the better we can project thier changes in future climate.

I am a Postdoctoral Researcher at the University of Vienna. I received my PhD from LMD-IPSL / ENS-PSL in Paris, I studies monsoon systems during my M.Sc in atmospheric sciences.

- **Postdoc**, [University of Vienna](https://flexteam.univie.ac.at/team/) (2025–2026): moisture and heat transport in heatwaves, Horizon Europe project Edu4ClimAte.
- **PhD**, [LMD-IPSL](https://www.lmd.ipsl.fr/) / [ENS-PSL](https://www.ens.psl.eu/), Paris (2021–2024): atmospheric blocking and temperature extremes. Marie Skłodowska-Curie fellow ([EDIPI](https://edipi-itn.eu/)), with research stays at Imperial College London and Uppsala University.
- **Outreach:** co-organised the *How to read a storm* workshop at the [Vienna Klima Biennale](https://sophiedyer.net/teaching-and-learning/how-to-read-a-storm-klima-biennale/), and the atmospheric transport modeling Autumn School for [Edu4ClimAte](https://emme-care.cyi.ac.cy/the-2025-advanced-atmospheric-observations-data-mining-modelling-autumn-school-has-been-successfully-concluded/). Started a weekly weather discussion group at the University of Vienna.
- **Machine learning projects:** [which blocking patterns bring heatwaves](https://github.com/Vinita-D/Blocking_to_heatwaves_ML).
