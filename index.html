Vinita Deshmukh

Atmospheric scientist | Weather and climate extremes | Machine learning for weather and climate (developing)

🥗⚽ This is me: eating healthy, in my Reve 1988 football-match winner shirt

Atmospheric scientist with almost five years of research on the large-scale weather patterns behind European weather and climate extremes, climate and weather data analysis, Lagrangian modelling (FLEXPART, LAGRANTO), climate and weather modelling (GCM, ICON) and machine learning skill and tools.

Postdoctoral researcher (2025–2026) at the Department of Meteorology and Geophysics, University of Vienna, in the Horizon Europe project Edu4ClimAte. I developed a Lagrangian moist static energy framework that attributes heatwave intensity to surface fluxes and transported air masses.

PhD in atmospheric dynamics (2024) at the Laboratoire de Météorologie Dynamique (CNRS/ENS-PSL, Paris), as Marie Skłodowska-Curie fellow in the EDIPI network, on atmospheric blocking and its link to temperature extremes, with research stays at Imperial College London and Uppsala University.

Building Machine learning skills. Blocking_to_heatwaves_ML: which atmospheric blocks bring heatwaves (scikit-learn). Quasi-Lagrangian heatwaves: open code to detect and track heatwaves, archived on Zenodo.

Science communication. Organiser of a weekly weather discussion group at the University of Vienna, organiser of the workshop How to read storms at the Vienna Klima Biennale, co-organiser of the Edu4ClimAte Autumn School (Crete), and initiator of the Café des Géosciences at LMD-ENS.

What I am looking for. A place where physical understanding of weather and climate meets AI and machine learning, to work on real-world problems and make a meaningful contribution. Curiosity brought me here, and I want to keep learning.

Outside research: tennis, hiking and dancing. I love living in Europe, discovering each country’s unique culture and blending in. My French (A2) and German (A1) are works in progress, just like my tennis backhand.
